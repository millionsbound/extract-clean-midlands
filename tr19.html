What TR19 extract cleaning means

TR19 Grease is the UK specification for managing fire risk from grease in commercial kitchen extract systems. Here is what that means in practice, in plain language.

What TR19 Grease is

TR19 Grease is published by the Building Engineering Services Association (BESA). It is a stand-alone specification, first issued in that form in 2019, for the fire risk management of grease that builds up inside kitchen extraction systems.

BESA describes it as the definitive specification for this work. It covers inspection, cleaning and record-keeping for commercial kitchen extract ventilation, and it is used on new systems as well as on existing ones that need to be maintained.

There is a related specification, TR19 Air, which is about the cleanliness of general ventilation systems. Kitchen extract work is covered by TR19 Grease.

How often systems should be cleaned

BESA's published guidance on kitchen extract cleaning sets minimum frequencies based on how many hours the kitchen runs each day:

Kitchen useHours of cooking a dayMinimum clean
Heavy12 to 16 hoursEvery 3 months
Moderate6 to 12 hoursEvery 6 months
Light2 to 6 hoursEvery 12 months
 Minimum frequencies as set out in BESA guidance on TR19 kitchen extract cleaning. A fire risk assessment, insurer or landlord may ask for more frequent cleaning. The type of cooking also matters.

These are baselines, not a guarantee that a system is safe until the next date. Actual frequency should be reviewed against how quickly grease returns, and against any requirements in your fire risk assessment or insurance policy.

How clean is clean enough

BESA's guidance on TR19 Grease says grease levels should be controlled so they do not exceed a mean average of 200 microns between scheduled cleans. Areas showing deposits of 500 microns or more need immediate local cleaning. Measurements are taken with a deposit thickness test or wet film thickness test at points inside the ductwork.

A thorough clean is not just a wipe of the visible canopy. BESA's guidance is that work should cover the canopy, filters, the length of the ductwork, the fan and the discharge point, with proper access panels so hidden sections can be reached.

What you should get after the job

After a compliant clean, the contractor should give you a report you can keep for your fire risk records and, if asked, for your insurer. BESA's guidance typically includes:

  • before and after photographs taken from the same positions
  • deposit thickness test results where they were carried out
  • a written summary of the work, including any access limits
  • a hygiene or post-clean certificate with the date, location and the name of the technician

Filters on the canopy still need regular cleaning by the kitchen team. No filter catches all of the grease. That is why the duct behind the canopy has to be professionally cleaned as well.
